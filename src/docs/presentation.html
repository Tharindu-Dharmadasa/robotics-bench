Project Briefing

Robotics Bench:
3D Interactive Electronics & Fault Lab

A zero-backend, browser-based 3D hardware simulator where learners assemble real-world robotics boards component-by-component, inspect live electrical current flow, and safely test circuit failures in real time.

Hands-On Assembly

Step-by-step PCB assembly from bare board to component placement, soldering, USB power-up, and live firmware execution.

Real Failure Physics

Removing resistors, flyback diodes, or regulators triggers realistic arc flashes, sparks, smoke, and diagnostic explanations.

Open Experiment Sandbox

Includes 13 guided hardware modules plus a full-screen customizable breadboard workbench for free-form circuit prototyping.

01 · Project Requirements

Key Objectives Defined for the Build

Built to replace passive 2D circuit diagrams with realistic, interactive 3D hardware behavior.

Authentic Hardware Representation
  • Distinct physical form factors for every lab (notched Arduino PCB, solderless breadboard, perfboard, DevKitC-1, heatsinks, transparent servo gearbox, OLED glass).
  • Hybrid 3D view toggle supporting both interactive procedural parts and imported industrial CAD (.glb) models.
Interactive Testing & Diagnostics
  • Clickable Bill of Materials (BOM) to add, remove, or change component values (e.g., 220Ω vs 10Ω, VREF trimpot tuning).
  • Live interactive controls per lab (tilt sliders, rotary encoder knob, PWM sweep, ultrasonic distance target) paired with Serial Monitor telemetry.

03 · Educational Impact

How Users Learn by Experimenting

Every component is mapped to real circuit rules—giving instant visual and Serial Monitor feedback when something goes wrong.

Three Failure Severities
  • BURNOUT: Missing current limiters or flyback diodes cause overcurrent/back-EMF damage with 3D sparks and smoke.
  • HALT: Missing regulators, crystals, or pull-ups break circuit continuity and highlight the open pad in 3D.
  • DEGRADED: Missing decoupling or debounce capacitors introduce realistic signal noise and jitter.
> System Powered ON · Running Blink.ino
[0.5s] D13 HIGH · Current: 14.5 mA (Nominal)
! BURNOUT: 220Ω Current-Limiting Resistor removed!
! ERROR: Current spiked to 135 mA — LED destroyed.
[LEARNING NOTE]: Series resistance is required to limit forward current below the LED's 20 mA max rating.
